Movie Webpage Project

Content

After finishing our unit on HtNL and CSS, my SEP teacher, Mr. Mueller, assigned a large project: create a website about a nonexistent movie. We were given free reign over what the movie would be about, but there were obviously some requirements to demonstrate our knowledge of HTML and CSS. Additionally, we were required to work in partnerships, which tested our efficiency working in groups.

Content

As mentioned previously, we created a webpage about a fictitious movie using HMTL and CSS concepts that we learned previously. We had to use HTML elements, such as paragraphs, headings, divs, spans, inputs, submit buttons, images, and imported Google fonts. As for CSS, we had to use element selectors, class selectors, and ID selectors, as well as the padding and margin properties. Additionally, we were required to use markdown, a simple language similar to HTML, to create a plan, dividing tasks between ourselves and our partners.

Challenges

I found most of this project easy, as I was quite adept at HTML and CSS. My greatest struggle, however, was differentiating between margin and padding. I had struggled with this previously, and treated this project as a way to master it, but nevertheless I kept mixing up the two properties. By the end, however, I believe I have a far better grasp on these concepts than I used to. I also struggled with creating inputs and submit buttons. I took very poor notes on those concepts, and I refer back to my notes for information often, so I was stuck. Fortunately, I was able to combine the information from my notes and platforms like W3schools to create them for my website. Additionally, I was able to learn a lot of other concepts from this project, most of them having not been taught in class, such as animations, gradient backgrounds, and backgrounds with varying levels of opacity. All of these concepts I found challenging at first, but with some trial and error and effective Googling, I was able to master them fairly quickly.

As for myself and my partner's challenges, we struggled to divide tasks. Initially, our plan was sound, but as I am a perfectionist, I found myself fixing errors in my partner's work so much so that I was dipping into his tasks. It didn't help that we had a large gap in skill; my partner struggled with most of the HTML and CSS concepts we learned, while as previously mentioned, I was quite adept at them. I really wanted the project to be perfect, looking as good as possible and riddled with advanced concepts, while he was less invested in such a result. I regret nitpicking his work so much; I felt like it must have come off as calling him stupid. I need to work on being okay with results that are less than perfect, as it is neccessary to accept your groupmates' work on such a project.

Takeaways

As previously mentioned, I learned that I need to work on my perfectionism. Not only did it cause conflict with my partner, it also had me working on the project for hours longer than I needed to at home trying and failing to add advanced concepts that were far beyond my skill level. For big projects, especially those where I work in a group, being a perfectionist greatly reduces myself and my groupmates' efficiency. Additionally, it hurts my relationship with them, and once I go on to become a working adult that will be a significant problem. I'm not sure how I will remedy this trait, as I have had it for a long time, but I will try to be okay with imperfect results.

Next Steps

As a next step, I would like to add more images. My partner worked on adding them to the code, but I was the one editing them, and I had a lot of fun doing it. I would have loved to add an image of our movie's villain if I had the time.
